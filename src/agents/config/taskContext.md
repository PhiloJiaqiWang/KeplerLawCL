# Task Context

## Kepler First Law

Learners investigate whether a planet's orbit is better described as a circle or an ellipse.

### Task Goal
- Use distance measurements across orbit points to compare circular and elliptical models.
- Determine whether the evidence supports an orbit with the star at a focus.

### Available Tools
- `Distance Tool`: measures distances from selected orbit points to targets such as the center, Focus 1, and Focus 2.

### Productive Evidence
- Center distances that vary across orbit points weaken a circular model.
- Similar sums of distances to Focus 1 and Focus 2 across multiple points support an elliptical model.
- Multiple points are needed before making a claim.

## Kepler Second Law

Learners investigate how a planet's motion changes around its orbit and whether equal time intervals sweep out equal areas.

### Task Goal
- Compare speed and swept area measurements at different orbit locations.
- Determine whether changing speed still follows a consistent orbital rule.

### Available Tools
- `Speed Tool`: measures the planet's speed at selected orbit locations.
- `Swept Area Tool`: measures the area swept by the star-planet line over a selected time interval.

### Productive Evidence
- The planet moves faster near the star and slower farther from the star.
- Equal time intervals should produce similar swept areas.
- Speed evidence and swept-area evidence should be interpreted together.

## Kepler Third Law

Learners investigate how orbital period relates to semi-major axis across multiple planetary orbits around the same star.

### Task Goal
- Collect period and semi-major axis measurements for multiple orbits.
- Compare plotted relationships using different powers of `P` and `a`.
- Determine which relationship best captures the pattern across orbits.

### Available Tools
- `Period Tool`: measures how long a selected planet takes to complete one orbit.
- `Axis Tool`: measures the selected orbit's semi-major axis.
- `Pattern Plot View`: plots selected powers of semi-major axis and orbital period.

### Productive Evidence
- Larger semi-major axes correspond to longer orbital periods.
- Each orbit needs both a period and an axis measurement for comparison.
- The relationship is nonlinear, so comparing powers such as `P²` and `a³` can reveal the pattern.
